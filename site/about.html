About SWAMITRA

A national institution built to make legal knowledge ordinary

SWAMITRA Foundation is a not-for-profit institution working to make legal understanding a normal part of Indian life rather than a specialist possession. We design frameworks, deliver training and publish research for the institutions where daily life actually happens — schools, colleges, workplaces, government departments and community organisations.

Our national purpose is straightforward. The Constitution guarantees rights most citizens cannot name, and places duties on institutions that most institutions have never been taught to operationalise. That distance between the law as written and the law as lived is not a failure of drafting; it is a failure of education and of institutional design. SWAMITRA exists to close it, at national scale, with one consistent standard that can be adapted by every state, board and sector.

Institutionally, the Foundation is constituted as a charitable body with a formal Objects Clause of sixty charitable objects grouped into six missions — constitutional and legal, education and capacity building, research and knowledge, public welfare, institutional development, and future readiness. Every programme, course and publication we run must trace back to one of those objects. That is a deliberate discipline: it keeps the Foundation from drifting into whatever happens to be fundable in a given year.

It is equally important to be clear about what we are not. SWAMITRA is not a litigation body, a political organisation or a campaign group. We do not represent clients, take positions in electoral politics or run awareness drives that end when the funding does. We are a knowledge and capacity institution: we produce evidence, turn it into usable standards, and build the people who can run those standards without us.

Our Story

Why the Foundation was established

SWAMITRA began with a question that kept returning in classrooms, staff rooms and human resources offices: why do the people most affected by a law almost never understand it?

The founding conviction is simple. A student who cannot name a single fundamental right, a teacher who does not know what to do when a child discloses harm, a committee member appointed two years ago and never trained — each of these is an avoidable failure, and each of them repeats itself thousands of times a day across the country. India does not lack law. It lacks the education, the standards and the trained people that turn law into practice.

The Foundation was established to work on that gap as an institution rather than as a campaign. That meant starting with a constitution of its own — an Objects Clause broad enough to cover the whole problem and specific enough to hold us to it — and then building outward in a fixed order: evidence, framework, training, partnership, measurement.

We chose to work through existing institutions from the beginning. Schools, universities, employers and government departments already have the authority, the reach and the relationships. What they usually lack is a standard they can adopt, material they can trust and people they can send for training. Supplying those three things is more useful, and far more durable, than running parallel programmes of our own.

We also chose to publish everything we can. Frameworks, toolkits, templates and research are released free of cost and free to adapt with attribution, because a safety standard that sits behind a paywall protects nobody.

Journey and milestones

  1. The Foundation is constituted — 2025

    SWAMITRA is established as a not-for-profit institution and adopts its Objects Clause: sixty charitable objects across six missions, drafted so that education, research, safety and governance work sit under one legal mandate.

  2. Research before programmes — 2025

    The first baseline studies on constitutional literacy and institutional safety are commissioned, on the principle that no framework should be published until we can show what problem it solves.

  3. The first national framework is published — 2026

    The National Safe School Framework v1.0 is released free of charge, giving schools a single auditable standard covering policy, committees, physical and digital safety, incident response and annual review.

  4. The first master trainer cohort is certified — 2026

    The Train the Trainer programme certifies its first cohort, so that participating institutions and regions can deliver SWAMITRA programmes independently rather than waiting for us.

  5. The Resource Centre opens to the public — 2026

    Toolkits, policy templates, standard operating procedures and checklists are published for free download and adaptation, with regional-language versions added progressively.

  6. The Research & Policy Centre begins publishing — 2026

    The first baseline study, white paper and policy briefs are released, establishing the evidence base that later frameworks and training curricula are built on.

Where we are going

The next phase is depth rather than noise. We would rather one state implement the Safe School Framework properly than twenty states announce it. Our medium-term commitment is to hold quality constant while reach grows: the same standard, the same training rigour and the same published evidence, whether the institution is a metropolitan university or a single-teacher rural school.

  • Regional-language editions of every core framework and toolkit
  • Certified master trainers in every region we work in, so capability stays local
  • Independent audit and measurement of what our frameworks actually change
  • Long-term research partnerships with universities and public institutions

Vision & Mission

Building a Constitutionally Aware, Legally Empowered and Civically Responsible India.

Our vision describes the country we are working towards. Our mission describes the six things we do to get there — each one measurable, each one delivered through institutions.

Vision

A Constitutionally Aware, Legally Empowered and Civically Responsible India.

An India where the Constitution is understood as a working guide to everyday rights, duties and choices — not as a distant document quoted on ceremonial occasions.

Mission

Strengthening legal awareness, institutional safety, civic participation and constitutional values through education, research and partnerships.

The mission is delivered through six commitments. Each has its own research base, framework, curriculum and resource library, and each is designed to be run by the institution itself once training is complete.

Our six mission commitments

01

Advance Legal Education

Make structured legal learning available at every stage of education — school modules, university electives, professional courses and community sessions — so that legal competence stops being confined to the legal profession.

02

Promote Constitutional Literacy

Teach the Constitution as a working document: its philosophy, its institutions, and the rights and duties it places in the hands of every citizen and every institution that serves them.

03

Build Safe Schools

Give every school one coherent, auditable standard covering physical safety, child protection, digital conduct and emotional wellbeing — together with the staff training and review cycle that keep it alive.

04

Build Safe Workplaces

Help organisations move from a policy on paper to a working system: correctly constituted committees, trained members, genuine employee awareness and records that stand up to scrutiny.

05

Strengthen Child Protection

Equip every adult around a child to recognise risk, receive a disclosure without causing further harm, and report through the correct pathway without delay or improvisation.

06

Produce Research and Policy Frameworks

Generate the national evidence base — surveys, working papers, standards and policy briefs — that allows institutions and policymakers to act on facts rather than impressions.

Core Values

Seven values, applied to ourselves first

These are the tests we apply to our own decisions — what we publish, whose money we accept, and how we behave when we are wrong.

Integrity

We claim only what we can evidence, keep the commitments we make to institutions, and correct our errors publicly rather than quietly.

Inclusion

Programmes are designed first for the people usually reached last — rural institutions, regional-language learners, women's groups and persons with disabilities.

Excellence

Free does not mean rough. Every framework, module and template is built to a professional standard and reviewed before release.

Constitutional Values

Justice, liberty, equality, fraternity and human dignity govern how we run the Foundation, not only what we teach in its classrooms.

Transparency

Our objects, policies, governance arrangements and finances are published. Anyone should be able to see how the Foundation is run and how it is funded.

Collaboration

We work through existing institutions rather than around them, and we share authorship, data and credit with the partners who do the work with us.

Innovation

We adopt new methods and technologies where they measurably improve reach or quality — and decline them where they only improve appearances.

Our Approach

Research first, then everything else

Every area of the Foundation's work follows the same five-stage sequence. It is deliberately slow at the start, which is what makes it fast and durable at the end.

ResearchEstablish what is actually happening through surveys, field studies and institutional data, before proposing anything.
EducationTurn the evidence into curricula, frameworks and learning material that an institution can adopt without rewriting it.
Capacity BuildingTrain the people who will deliver it — teachers, committee members, leaders and master trainers.
Institutional PartnershipsEmbed the work inside schools, universities, employers and government bodies so it survives a change of leadership.
Community ImpactMeasure what changed for students, employees and communities — and publish it, including where it did not work.

The sequence matters more than any single stage. Skipping research produces frameworks that describe a problem nobody has. Skipping capacity building produces beautiful documents that no one is able to implement. Skipping partnership produces pilots that end with the project. Skipping measurement produces confidence without evidence, which is the most expensive outcome of all.

In practice this means we say no fairly often — to launches without a baseline, to training without follow-up, and to reach figures that count attendance rather than change.

Governance

How decisions are made, recorded and checked

Governance at SWAMITRA is designed to be boring in the best sense: predictable meetings, written decisions, declared interests and an audit trail that outlives the people who created it.

Governance structure

The Foundation operates a three-tier structure. The Board of Directors holds ultimate responsibility for the charitable objects. Board committees carry out detailed scrutiny in their areas. The executive team delivers within a written delegation of authority.

  • Board of Directors — strategy, budget, risk and appointments
  • Board committees — audit, programme quality, research ethics, safeguarding
  • Executive team — delivery within a documented delegation of authority

Committees

Each committee has written terms of reference, a defined composition and a reporting line to the Board. Statutory committees, including the Internal Committee and the safeguarding function, are constituted and trained before any related activity begins.

  • Written terms of reference, reviewed annually
  • Independent membership where the subject requires it
  • Minutes recorded and reported to the next Board meeting

Decision-making framework

Decisions are classified by value and by risk. Routine operational decisions sit with the executive; anything that commits the Foundation financially, publicly or reputationally beyond a defined threshold goes to the Board or its committee.

  • Every material decision is recorded in writing with its rationale
  • Conflicts of interest are declared and the interested person recuses
  • Research and advisory outputs are not influenced by funding relationships

Risk & compliance

The Foundation maintains a risk register covering safeguarding, statutory compliance, financial control, data protection and reputational risk. It is reviewed by the executive monthly and by the Board quarterly.

  • Safeguarding and child protection treated as the highest-priority risk category
  • Statutory filings, audits and policy reviews tracked on a compliance calendar
  • Whistleblower channel available to staff, volunteers and partners

Financial information and CSR compliance

Annual accounts and audit

Accounts are maintained on a financial year running from 1 April to 31 March and are audited by an independent statutory auditor. Audited statements are published with the Annual Report and presented to the Board through the Audit & Finance Committee.

Utilisation of funds

Funds are applied only to the charitable objects set out in the Objects Clause. Programme, training, research and administration costs are reported separately so that the balance between delivery and overhead is visible.

Donations and grants

Donations are accepted only through authorised channels and are receipted. The Foundation may decline any contribution that conflicts with its values, its independence or its legal obligations.
