Featured Resources

Six kinds of document, six different jobs

Knowing which type of document you actually need saves a great deal of time. Each type is written to do one thing well.

Toolkits

Complete implementation kits. A toolkit takes a framework and breaks it into sequenced steps, with the forms, session plans and review points needed at each stage.

Templates

Editable documents you adapt and adopt — policies, committee constitutions, consent formats, registers and reporting formats. Adapt them to your context before adoption.

Checklists

Audit and readiness instruments. A checklist tells you whether a requirement is met, partially met or absent, so that a claim of compliance can be evidenced rather than asserted.

SOPs

Standard operating procedures for situations where hesitation causes harm — receiving a disclosure, responding to an incident, escalating a concern, recording what was done.

Policy Documents

Model policies covering child protection, workplace dignity, governance and ethics, written to sit alongside statutory obligations rather than restate them loosely.

Handbooks

Reference material for the person delivering the work — educators, committee members and community facilitators — with explanation, examples and common errors.

    File formats and licence terms

    Format follows function. A document you must edit is never shipped as a locked file, and a document that must not be altered is not shipped as an editable one.

    File formats offered in the Resource Centre
    FormatUsed for
    PDFFrameworks, handbooks, SOPs, reports and infographics — fixed text that should be cited and printed as published.
    DOCXPolicy and document templates intended to be edited, adapted and adopted under your own institution's name.
    PPTSession plans and briefing decks for trainers, faculty and management presentations.
    XLSXAudit checklists, compliance trackers and assessment instruments that calculate or score.
    ZIPMulti-file kits — poster sets, activity toolkits and template packs supplied as a bundle.

    Licence and attribution

    Everything published here is free for educational and non-commercial use, provided SWAMITRA Foundation is clearly credited and the meaning of the content is not altered. You may print, circulate internally, translate for your own institution's use, and adapt a template to your context.

    Commercial reproduction, resale, rebranding or derivative distribution requires prior written permission. Where a document contains third-party material, those rights remain with the respective owners.

    Templates and toolkits are educational resources, not legal advice. Adapt them to your circumstances and have them reviewed by a qualified professional before formal adoption.

    Request a resource

    If the document you need does not exist yet, tell us. Requests are read, and repeated requests shape what the Foundation publishes next.

    • Ask for a document that is listed but not yet downloadable
    • Request a translation into a specific Indian language
    • Ask for an accessible version of a document you cannot use
    • Propose material that your sector needs and nobody has produced
    • Request permission for a use the licence does not already cover

    Requests are acknowledged on receipt. Where a document already exists we will send the link; where it does not, we will tell you honestly whether it is planned.

    Telling us the setting — school, workplace, community group — helps us send the right version.
