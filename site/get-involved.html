Volunteer Programme

Give the hours you actually have

Volunteers deliver sessions, translate material, support camps, help with research and keep our resources current. Every volunteer is trained before being placed.

Eligibility

  • Eighteen years or older
  • Any educational background — a legal qualification is useful but not required
  • Willingness to complete the volunteer orientation before placement
  • Comfort working in at least one Indian language, English included
  • Agreement to the Code of Ethics and the Child Protection Policy
  • Clearance requirements apply to anyone working directly with children

Areas of contribution

  • Facilitating legal awareness and constitutional literacy sessions
  • Supporting community legal camps and helpdesk clinics
  • Translating and reviewing material in regional languages
  • Assisting research — surveys, data entry, literature review
  • Helping schools with safety audits and policy documentation
  • Communications, design, photography and documentation
  • Event coordination and participant support

Volunteer benefits

  • Free access to SWAMITRA training modules relevant to your placement
  • A dated certificate of volunteering stating hours and area of work
  • Named credit on any material you help produce
  • Reimbursement of approved travel and material costs for field work
  • A reference letter after six months of sustained contribution
  • Priority consideration for master trainer and fellowship tracks

How the placement works

You register below. We read every registration and respond within five working days, including when the answer is that we have nothing suitable right now. If there is a fit, you are invited to a short conversation, then to the orientation session for your area.

Placements are made against real requirements — a school that needs a facilitator, a camp that needs a translator, a study that needs field support. We do not create work to occupy volunteers, and we will tell you plainly if the need in your city has not yet arisen.

Most volunteers give between four and twelve hours a month. Some give a single day to a camp. Both are useful; neither is more welcome than the other.

Anyone volunteering in a child-facing role must complete child protection training and the screening process before their first session. There are no exceptions to this, including for short placements.

Volunteer registration

We respond to every registration within five working days. Your details are handled in line with our Privacy Policy.

Partnership Opportunities

We work through institutions, not around them

A partnership with SWAMITRA is a working arrangement with named deliverables, a timeline and a review point. It is not a logo exchange.

Government

State education departments, district administrations, commissions and public sector undertakings adopt our frameworks at scale.

  • Adoption of the Safe School or Safe Workplace standard across a district or department
  • Master trainer development inside the department so delivery continues without us
  • Curriculum and material support in the official language of the state
  • Baseline and end-line measurement with a published report

Schools & Universities

Schools, school chains, colleges, universities and law schools implement legal literacy and safety frameworks on their own campuses.

  • Graded classroom modules and teacher facilitation support

  • Campus legal aid cells, student clubs and clinical legal education support

  • Shared documentation and joint reporting on reach and outcomes

How it starts

The first conversation

Send the enquiry below. We come back within seven working days with an honest assessment of whether the partnership is workable, what it would require from each side, and what it would cost — including when the answer is that it costs nothing.

Every partnership is documented in a written memorandum before work begins, with named deliverables, a timeline and a review date.

What we ask of a partner

A named person who owns the relationship on your side. Access to the people the programme is meant to reach. Permission to measure honestly, including when the results are unflattering. And a willingness to continue after the first year, because nothing in this field changes in one.

What we do not ask for is exclusivity, endorsement of positions you have not read, or the right to use your name in fundraising material without your written agreement.

What you can expect from us

  • A written memorandum before any work begins
  • Material free of cost wherever the Foundation can bear it, and transparent cost recovery where it cannot
  • Measurement designed before delivery, not retrofitted afterwards
  • Capability transferred to your own people, so the work survives our exit

Donation

What a contribution actually pays for

Everything the Foundation publishes is free to download and use. Contributions are what make that possible.

Please note: donation channels will be published once the Foundation's payment gateway and statutory registrations are live. We are not collecting card, bank or UPI details on this website, and no page here will ever ask you for them. If you would like to contribute now, please write to us and we will discuss it directly.
Tier 1

Supporting a classroom

Funds a printed module set and workbook for one classroom for a full academic year, including the teacher facilitation guide.

Tier 2

Supporting a school

Covers a school's baseline safety audit, policy adoption support and one staff training day under the Safe School Framework.

Tier 3

Supporting a community camp

Funds a full community legal awareness camp — facilitator time, plain-language material, helpdesk clinic and one follow-up visit.

Tier 4

Supporting a master trainer

Puts one person through the full Train the Trainer intensive and practicum, creating capability that outlasts any single grant.

One-time donation

A single contribution, applied to the tier or programme you name. If you do not name one, it goes to the general programme fund and is reported as such.

Monthly giving

A recurring contribution is what lets us commit to a school or district for a full year rather than a single term. It is the most useful form of support we receive.

Campaign donations

Time-bound appeals tied to a specific target — a district rollout, a translation into a particular language, a survey in a named state. Each campaign publishes its target and its closing report.

Transparency & utilisation

Aggregate utilisation is published in the Annual Report. Donors may ask how their contribution category was applied and we will answer with figures, not adjectives.

Utilisation and receipts, stated plainly

Contributions are applied to the Foundation's charitable objects — programmes, training, research, resource production and community initiatives. A share necessarily goes to the administration that keeps those activities lawful and accountable; we report that share rather than pretending it is zero.

A receipt is issued for every contribution. Tax exemption benefits depend on the Foundation's registration status under applicable law, and we will not claim a benefit is available until the relevant registration is in force. When it is, we will say so here with the date it took effect.

Donors may ask that their contribution remain unpublished. Statutory reporting obligations continue to apply, and we will tell you where they do.

Talk to us before you give

We would rather have a short conversation about what you want your contribution to do than accept it blind. Tell us what you care about — a state, a school type, a subject — and we will tell you honestly whether we can put money to work there.

CSR Collaboration

Corporate social responsibility with a measurable outcome

CSR partnerships fund defined work in a defined place, with reporting a board can actually rely on.

Proposal process

EnquiryYou tell us your CSR focus, budget window and preferred geography.
ScopingWe assess need in that geography and propose what can realistically be achieved.
ProposalA written proposal with deliverables, timeline, budget and measurement design.
AgreementA signed memorandum covering scope, reporting, branding and audit rights.
DeliveryImplementation against the agreed plan, with a named point of contact on both sides.
ReportingQuarterly progress notes and a closing impact report with baseline and end-line data.

Impact reporting

Every CSR engagement is measured against a baseline taken before delivery starts. We report participation, completion and assessed learning change — not just the number of people in a room. Where an indicator did not move, the report says so and explains what we think went wrong.

Reports are written to be usable in your own statutory CSR disclosures, with the methodology stated so an auditor can follow it. Underlying data is retained and can be inspected on request.

We do not accept engagements where the reporting requirement is a photograph and a headcount. If that is the deliverable, we are the wrong partner and will say so at the scoping stage.

Before you send a proposal

  • Tell us the geography — district level is more useful than state level
  • Tell us the budget window, even approximately, so we can scope honestly
  • Tell us your board's reporting cycle, so measurement fits it
  • Tell us whether employee volunteering is part of the intent

Internship

Real work, supervised, with something to show for it

Interns contribute to live research, curriculum and programme work. Nobody is given filing to do for eight weeks.

Eligibility

  • Students in any undergraduate or postgraduate programme
  • Recent graduates within one year of completion
  • Law, social sciences, education, public policy, communications and data backgrounds are all relevant
  • A demonstrated interest in the domain you apply for

Available domains

  • Research & Policy
  • Curriculum & Content Development
  • Programme Delivery & Field Support
  • Communications & Design
  • Data & Monitoring
  • Legal & Compliance Support

Duration

Internships run for four, eight or twelve weeks, full time or part time, remote or in person depending on the domain. Field-based internships require in-person availability for at least part of the term.

Start dates are grouped into cohorts so that induction, supervision and review happen properly rather than ad hoc.

Certificate

Every intern who completes the term and submits the final deliverable receives a dated certificate stating the domain, duration and the work produced.

Interns are named on any published material they contributed to. Outstanding interns are invited to continue as volunteers or to apply for fellowship tracks.

What the term actually looks like

You are assigned a supervisor and a single substantive deliverable — a literature review, a module draft, a district data set, a communications asset — sized to the length of your term. You meet your supervisor weekly. At the end you present the work and receive written feedback you can use.

Internships are unpaid at present. Approved travel and material costs for field work are reimbursed. We state this plainly rather than at the end of the process, so you can decide before investing time in an application.

Interns placed in child-facing field work must complete child protection training and screening before any contact with children.

Internship application

Applications are reviewed at the start of each cohort. Your details are handled in line with our Privacy Policy.

Equal opportunity statement

SWAMITRA Foundation selects and promotes people on the basis of merit and the requirements of the role, and on nothing else. We do not discriminate on grounds of religion, race, caste, sex, gender identity, sexual orientation, place of birth, language, descent, marital status, pregnancy, age, disability or economic background, whether in recruitment, remuneration, training, promotion or termination.

We ask about a candidate's requirements rather than assuming them, and we make reasonable adjustments to the application process, the interview and the workplace so that a disabled candidate is assessed on the work and not on the arrangements. Our selection panels record the reason for every shortlisting decision, so that reasoning can be reviewed.

We hold ourselves to the same standards we ask of the institutions we train. Anyone who believes a recruitment decision at the Foundation was made on a prohibited ground may raise it under our Whistleblower Policy, and it will be examined.
